# The Grand Granny Rooster Joust — Game Guide

*Every tab, every rule, every number that matters. The same guide is in the game under **More → 📖 Game Guide**.*

---

## 1. Welcome

You're a granny on a rooster, starting at **#1000 of 1,000** on the world jousting tour. Every week you ride in a tournament, win matches with well-timed strikes, earn ranking points, prize money and training, and climb — from the dusty Futures lanes to the golden helmets of the top ten.

Every one of the 999 other grannies is a full rider too: she plays her own tournaments, earns points, trains by the same rules as you, and has her own face, rooster, trait and opinions.

> 📣 The whole game rests on one skill: stopping the moving marker on the centre of the gold zone. Everything else — training, money, fans — shifts the odds, but your timing decides matches.

## 2. Getting around

| Tab | What's there |

| 🏠 **Home** | Your granny card (tap it for your profile), the next event and its button, your rival, achievements, the latest newspaper and your ranking chart. |
| 🏋️ **Train** | Spend training points on six stats, this week's investment (private coach, physio, pedigree), and how you compare with the riders near your rank. A red badge shows how many stats you can afford. |

| ⚙️ **More** | Your account, the world leaderboard, the game guide, achievements, replaying the tutorial, the rules, music and sound switches, and saves. |

## 3. The season

A season is **20 events** in four blocks. Each block is four weekly tournaments followed by a **Grand Slam**. After the 20th event the top 16 ride in the **Season Finals**, then the season ends with a gala.

**Ranking points and defending.** Your world rank is your *season points*. Points carry over into the next season, but the points you won at a given week last season **drop off** when that week comes round again. You "defend" them: win more than last time and you climb, less and you slip. The Season tab shows what you're defending, in red.

**Grand Slams.** Only the **top 64** may enter, or anyone with **80+ fans** (a fan wildcard). If you can't enter, you sit the week out. Slams have 64 riders, best of 5 sets, and enormous points.

**Season Finals.** The top 16 after event 20 are split into four houses of four (🔥 Phoenix, 🌊 Kraken, 🐉 Wyvern, ⚡ Chimera). Everyone plays everyone in her house (3 points a win); the four house winners meet in the semifinals and then the World Final. Every Finals match is best of 5. If you don't qualify, the Finals are played without you and a world champion is crowned all the same.

## 4. Tournaments

Tap **Choose tournament** on Home or Season. Your rank decides which tiers are open:

| Tier | Open to ranks | Sets | Champion's points | Champion's prize |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Futures | 601–1000 | Best of 3 | 192 | $4.6K |
| Challenger | 301–700 | Best of 3 | 477 | $16K |
| Tour 250 | 101–400 | Best of 3 | 1,200 | $64K |
| Tour 500 | 31–200 | Best of 3 | 2,360 | $162K |
| Masters | 1–100 | Best of 5 | 4,750 | $460K |
| Grand Slam | 1–64 | Best of 5 | 15,750 | $1.6M |

Each tournament is a knockout bracket (32 riders, 64 at a Slam). Every round you win adds points and prize money; the totals above are for winning the whole thing. Lose and your week is over.

**Named events: pick your week**

Each week several tournaments run at once in every tier, and all 1,000 riders play one of them. Tap a tier to see its events. Each card shows:
- **Name and lanes.** The conditions change the match for both riders:
  - ☀️ **Classic:** no tricks.
  - 🌧️ **Muddy lanes:** bar 10% faster for both riders, and Reflexes gaps count double.
  - 🌬️ **Windy coast:** gold zone 2% narrower for both, Precision gaps count double, +10% prize money.
  - 🔥 **Heatwave:** the bar speeds up 40% more each strike, Stamina gaps count double.
  - 🌼 **Village green:** gold zone 1.5% wider, every stat gap counts half (an underdog's day out), −10% points and money.
- **Entrants (★).** Who signed up:
  - ★★★ tough entrants: mostly the tier's top-ranked riders, +10% points.
  - ★★ mixed entrants.
  - ★ weaker entrants: mostly lower-ranked riders, −10% points.
- **Top seeds.** The two best-ranked riders in the draw, and what the champion takes home.

Grand Slam weeks have a single named Slam (The Coop Open, The Swamp Slam, Wimbledon-on-Wold, The Gale Cup), each with its own lanes.

**Getting into a higher tier early**
- **Fan wildcard:** with 70+ fans the tier above your normal range opens for free.
- **Paid entry:** at the bottom of the tournament list you can buy your way into the tier above for this week only (it uses this week's investment).

During a tournament the header shows the tier, the round, best-of and the bracket path. **Scout** opens the opponent's full report; 🌐 opens the bracket.

## 5. The match

**1 · The scouting report.** Before every match a "tale of the tape" compares you side by side: world rank, season points, her talent, every stat (green where you're stronger, red where she is), the difficulty, your head-to-head and her rooster. A red banner marks your arch-rival. Take your time, then tap **To the lanes!**

**2 · The opening.** The VS splash plays in full, then 3–2–1 and the announcer's **FIGHT!** Before a **final** (only a final) you first get a special build-up: the trophy, your road to the final, and your opponent revealed from the shadows.

**3 · Sets and strikes**
- A match is played in **sets**; each set is **3 strikes**.
- Each strike, you and she both score. The set goes to whoever has the **higher average** of the three — one bad strike can be rescued.
- Win the majority of sets: best of 3 (first to 2) up to Tour 500, best of 5 (first to 3) at Masters, Slams and the Finals. A deciding set only happens if it's needed.

**4 · Striking.** A blue marker sweeps back and forth across the bar. Tap the big round **STRIKE** button (or the arena, or press Space) when it's in the gold zone — as close to the white centre line as you can. The bar gets a little faster with every strike and every set.

**5 · Reading the screen**
- The chip at the top shows the set and strike. On the last strike of a set it turns into *Set point*, *Match point*, *Save the match* or *All on the line*, with the score you need.
- Coloured orbs under each name are that set's strikes; the bar below them is the set's running gap.
- Your strike leaves a tick on the bar, hers a red ▲. Between sets a break screen shows the set result.
- When the match is decided, the loser is unseated and the outro shows the score, points and prize.

> 📣 Progress is saved after every strike. Close the game mid-match and you'll pick up exactly where you left off.

## 6. Scoring

Both of you are scored the same way: **where the lance lands on the bar.**

| Where you land | Base score |
|---|---|
| On the centre line | 100 |
| Inside the gold zone | Slides from 100 down to 85 at the zone's edge |
| Just outside the gold | A sharp drop to 70 |
| Further out | Falls about 2 points for every % of the bar, down to your floor |

On top of the base score:
- **Aim** adds points to every strike — 1.5 per level you're ahead of her (or takes them away if she has more Aim). This is why a strike can show, say, 83: a gold-edge 85 minus a little Aim, or a just-outside 70 plus a lot of Aim.
- **Stubbornness** sets your floor — the lowest a strike can score (15 at even levels, ±1.5 per level of gap).
- There's almost no luck: ±1 point. The same strike always scores about the same.
- Labels: **PERFECT!** 96+, **GOLD!** inside the zone, **CLOSE** just outside, **MISS** beyond.

**How your opponent scores.** She strikes on the same kind of bar and is scored by the same rule. Her **talent** decides how tightly she groups around the centre — a gifted granny lands near 100 time after time, a developing one sprays all over. The round's speed spreads her out just as it does you.

**The crowd's roar.** On the last strike of a set with the match on the line, a crowd that loves you widens your gold zone: **+1%** of the bar with 60+ fans, **+2%** with 85+.

## 7. Difficulty

| What | What it does |
|---|---|
| 1 · The stat gap | Each stat is your level minus hers. Even levels cancel out. Ahead: a slower bar, a wider zone, extra points. Behind: the opposite. |
| 2 · The round | Each tier starts a step harder, and each round within a tournament adds a little more. This narrows the gold zone and speeds the bar for both of you, whatever your stats. |
| 3 · Her talent | How accurate she is. It doesn't touch your bar — it decides how high her scores tend to be. |

Example — the width of your gold zone with Steady Stride:

| Situation | Gold zone |
|---|---|
| Futures round 1, even levels | 13% of the bar |
| Same, you 5 levels ahead | 19% |
| Same, she's 5 levels ahead | 7% |
| Masters final, even levels | about 3% (the minimum) |

The gold zone never shrinks below 3% of the bar, and the bar never gets impossibly fast or slow.

## 8. Stats & training

Five stats — three for the granny, two for the rooster — each from level 0 to 20. **Only the gap to your opponent counts**: if you both have 5 Aim, it cancels out.

| Stat | What it does | Each level ahead |
|---|---|---|
| ⚡ Reflexes | The marker sweeps slower | Bar about 3–4% slower |
| 🎯 Aim | Extra points on every strike | +1.5 points per strike |
| 🛡️ Stubbornness | Your bad strikes aren't as bad | Floor +1.5 points |
| 🫀 Stamina | The bar speeds up less as the match goes on | About 5% less speed-up |
| 🐾 Steady Stride | A wider gold zone | +1.2% of the bar |

**Training points (TP).** Everyone on tour — you and every rival — earns TP by the same rule every week: **8 for turning up, plus a third of the ranking points you won.** A deep run buys you a few levels on the riders who went out early; the edge fades once you move up to a tier where everyone is winning that much. You also get **sponsor TP** from your fans (one per 10 fans).

Each level costs more than the last: 15 TP for level 1, 30 for level 2, 45 for level 3… and from level 9 the price climbs faster still.

**On the Train tab**
- Each stat shows what it does, its exact per-level effect, and when it's worth training.
- The dark tick on each bar, and the "Vs rivals" line, show the average level of the riders ranked near you — ahead, behind or level.
- Every level you buy plays a quick gag: a swatted fly, a bullseye, a bounced pillow, a treadmill, a balance beam.

## 9. Lances & saddles

After you pick a tournament you land on **Get ready**. Choose a lance and a saddle from **your locker**, or buy new ones at **Mavis's shop**. Nothing is paid or used up until you press **Confirm & ride**, so change your mind as often as you like; ✕ goes back to the tournament list.

- The **🧹 Trusty Broom-Handle** and the **🫖 Tea-Cosy Saddle** are free, neutral and always yours.
- Everything else lasts a set number of tournaments (1, 2, 3 or 5). Each tournament you enter with it uses one, however many rounds you play.
- Prices follow the best tier you can enter: one tournament's worth costs about 6% of that tier's champion prize.
- Gear changes **your** bar only, never your opponent's. After each strike the breakdown shows its points as **Gear**.
- **Rivals shop too**, with prize money they actually won: each picks the kit she thinks suits the lanes (the tour's best judge it well, lower ranks guess), keeps a reserve, and books physio when worn out. Her kit shows in the tale of the tape.
- Every item helps one thing and hurts another, and the lanes shift the balance. Precise riders tend to love the needle; riders who miss a lot get more from the baguette.

| 🥖 Day-Old Baguette | +3 points on misses, −2 points on gold hits | 1 | Mud: +1 points on misses · Heat: +4 points on misses |
| 🪵 Grandpa's Oak Lance | Bar starts 9% faster, +2 points every strike | 5 | Wind: Gold zone 0.3% wider · Heat: Speeds up 12% more each strike |

## 10. Fitness & physio

You have **two fitness meters**, 0–100%: 👵 the granny's and 🐓 the rooster's. They're on Home, in the Get ready screen, in the match header and in the tale of the tape (hers too).

- **Every match tires both of you:** −9% granny and −11% rooster (best of 5: −13.5% and −16.5%). A deep run leaves you tired in the later rounds — and so is the rider who got there with you.
- **A tired granny:** the gold zone gets narrower (up to −4% at 0) and misses score less (up to −10). The strike breakdown shows it as **Tired**.
- **A tired rooster:** the bar starts faster (up to +18%) and speeds up more after every strike (up to +60%).
- **Fitness only comes back between tournaments:** 14% + 30% of whatever is missing after every tournament, plus 1.25% per level of 🔋 **Bounce-back**. A week off (a Grand Slam you don't play) recovers double. Only part comes back, so tiredness builds up for anyone who keeps going deep.
- **🔋 Bounce-back** is the sixth stat (0–20). Unlike the others it counts on its own, not against your opponent.
- **Physio staff** (Train tab, as the week's one investment): 👩‍⚕️ Nurse Nellie +35% granny, 🩺 Dr. Featherby the rooster chiropractor +35% rooster, 🛁 a spa weekend at Bingo-on-Sea +20% both — straight away.
- **Injuries:** ride on under 35% and every match risks one (up to 25% a match at 0). An injury knocks 2–3 levels off one or two stats for 2–3 tournaments: 🦵 pulled hamstring (Reflexes), 💪 jouster's elbow (Aim), 🪑 creaky back (Stubbornness), 🐾 sprained claw (Steady Stride), 🪽 pulled wing (Stamina), 🦴 bruised wishbone (both rooster stats). Physio on that side shortens it by a tournament.
- The Season Finals tire you too.
- Every rider on tour plays by the same fitness rules.

## 11. Prize money

Prize money buys **one investment per tournament week**. After you make one, the rest lock until you play that week's tournament. Prices follow the prizes at your level, so they stay meaningful as you climb.

| Investment | What you get | Price |
|---|---|---|
| 📣 Private session | +10 TP (Train tab) | 20% of a champion's prize at your top open tier — about $900 at Futures, $90K at Masters |
| 🎟️ Paid entry | The tier above your range, this week only (tournament list) | 35% of that tier's champion's prize — about $5.7K for Challenger |
| 🧬 Pedigree certificate | Raises the cap on Stamina and Steady Stride: 20 → 22 → 24 → 26 | $250K, $1M, $3M |

The world leaderboard counts everything you've ever earned, so spending never costs you places there.

## 12. Fans & the press

Your **fans** (❤️, 0–100, starting at 20) are how much the crowd loves you.

**What moves them**
- **Riding:** each win +1, a comeback (after losing the first set) +2, a real upset (beating someone ranked well above you) +2, each loss −1. At most +6 a week from riding, plus a title bonus (+4, +10 for a Slam). The closer you are to 100, the harder each point comes.
- **Interviews:** after every tournament the press asks a question.
- **Promises:** a bold answer is a promise for the next tournament — reach the semifinals or better and it's +6; fall short and it's −5.
- **Revenge:** beating your rival +3.
- **Drift:** each week fans drift a little towards a baseline set by your fame — 30 for most, 40 in the top 200, 50 in the top 50, 60 in the top 10.

**What they buy you**

| Fans | Perk |
|---|---|
| Any | Sponsor TP every week (one per 10 fans) |
| 60+ | Crowd roar: +1% gold zone on match-point strikes |
| 70+ | Wildcard into the tier above |
| 80+ | Wildcard into the Grand Slams |
| 85+ | Crowd roar: +2% |

**The press conference.** Pick one of three funny answers — cheeky digs at your rival, daft excuses, funny-but-sporting lines, or bold promises. Each outlet's audience has its own taste, nobody likes the same kind of joke twice in a row, and now and then an answer goes viral. There's no answer that always works.

## 13. The Daily Cluck

A new issue prints after every tournament, and you can read it any time in the News tab (with the archive of recent issues).
- **The front page** goes to the week's biggest story — a Slam champion, a new world #1, a Cinderella title, a giant-killing, or you if you earned it.
- **Your week:** points, prize money, your best win, who knocked you out, and your interview quote with the crowd's verdict.
- **The feud:** what your rival did this week and your head-to-head.
- **Around your ranking:** who's climbing and falling near you, and gym gossip.
- **World tour:** the top five and the week's biggest title.

When it opens after a tournament, tap **Fold the paper** to put it away.

## 14. Rivals

Your **arch-rival** is the granny you keep meeting — preferably the one who keeps beating you. She's picked from your head-to-head record after each tournament (at least two meetings and one loss to her).
- She shows on Home, with a red banner before your matches, a badge on her profile, and her own column in the paper.
- Beat her for **Sweet Revenge** (+3 fans).
- Lead her head-to-head by three and the feud is **settled** — she stops bothering you, and the next grudge can take her place.

## 15. Rankings & profiles

The Ranks tab lists all 1,000 riders by season points and opens on your own row, with 25 above and below ("show 25 more" to see further). Switch to **Top 50** to see the leaders. Golden helmets mark the top ten; the world #1 wears the crown.

**Profiles.** Tap any granny for her profile: her rider and rooster, trait and quote, ranks and points, natural talent and potential, training levels, titles, and your head-to-head — every match with its result in sets and each set's score.

Tap your own card on Home for your profile. The ✏️ buttons rename your granny and your rooster.

## 16. Achievements

**35 achievements** in six groups — on the tour, with the lance, the ladder, feuds, crowd & press, and the gym. A badge slides down from the top when you earn one; Home and More show your collection, with progress bars on the counted ones and how to earn the ones you're missing.

## 17. End of season

When the season is over, the **Grand Gala** plays: the world champion is crowned, the season's awards are handed out one envelope at a time (most titles, biggest climb, most training, your best quote, fan favourite — and the participation ribbon), your season in numbers, the hall of the golden helmets, and a goal for next season.

Your training, prize money, badges, fans, rival and newspaper archive all carry over into the new season — and so do last season's points, which you'll defend week by week.

## 19. Coach's tips

- Aim for the white line, not just the gold. 100 against 86 is the difference between winning and losing a set.
- If you keep overshooting, train Reflexes. If you land just next to the gold, train Steady Stride. If your misses are wild, Stubbornness.
- Long best-of-5 matches get faster as they go: Stamina keeps them under control.
- Watch the "Vs rivals" line — falling behind the riders near you is felt immediately.
- A title is worth far more training than a quarterfinal. Stay in a tier you can win before you jump up.
- Save the paid entry and wildcards for weeks you feel sharp.
- Keep your interviews varied — the crowd gets bored of the same joke.
- Promises are risky: only make a bold promise if you really expect a semifinal.
